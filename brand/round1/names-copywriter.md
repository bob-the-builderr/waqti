# Naming report: the "verbal behavior" lens

Round 1, copywriter review (I worked separately from the brand agency). Checked on 19 Nov 2026.

## 0. The lens

A delivery name wins when people say it without thinking. Egyptians say «هطلب كريم», not "I'll open the Careem app". Fawry won because «فوري» was already in every mouth before the company existed. So every name here answers one question: **by week two, will a 60-year-old woman in Fayoum and a 19-year-old in Cairo both use it inside a sentence?**

Four filters:
1. **It bends.** It turns into a verb or a set phrase: «لمّها»، «خليها على ___»، «يا ___».
2. **No second meaning** that is dirty, mocking, unlucky, political or religious.
3. **One Latin spelling, 7 letters or fewer.** I avoid ق, which this governorate reads three ways: q on the news, a glottal stop (') in Fayoum city, and a hard g in many villages. I also avoid ج (written G or J) and ث/ذ/ظ.
4. **It makes a promise** (speed, everything, nearness, "it's handled") without literally describing delivery.

I also followed the founder's rejection pattern. He killed people's names (أدهم، جواد، عنتر), "yes sir" words (حاضر) and slang clock words (دلوقتي), so I don't bring back any of those three families.

## 2. What the screen taught

- **The most natural delivery words often have the worst Latin.** جاي is exactly what a rider shouts up the stairs, and it can never be a logo.
- **Egyptian slang punishes nouns that sound innocent:** تكّة، حتّة، ماشية، سوقي، جُرسة. Each would become a joke on TikTok within a week.
- **Superstition matters in Fayoum.** عتبة and «على الوعد» carry old bad omens that older customers will hear at once.
- **Political memory is long.** أمانة still sounds like «أمانة السياسات» to anyone over 35.
- **Food words narrow the brand.** كبسة، طازة and لفّة would make the pharmacy feel out of place.

## 4. Top 5

### 1. لمّة · Lamma

**Why it wins.** The verb already exists: لمّ يلمّ, to gather. And what it gathers is our moat: **three shops in one trip**. A phone call can't «تلمّ» a pharmacy, a bakery and a grocery. The word is warm for every age: «لمّة العيلة»، «لمّة رمضان»، «لمّة الشلة». It also gives a pun for free: «لمّا» ("when"). The shadda in لمّة has three prongs (ّ), which are literally three heads joined in one stroke. The mark is called شدّة, "strength", so the "powerful" the founder wants is printed on the word itself. In Latin, LAMMA is 5 letters with a strong double-M in the middle.

**Taglines**
- «لمّها في مشوار واحد»
- «تلات محلات.. لمّة واحدة»
- «لمّا تعوز.. لمّة»

**Habit lines**
- A mother to her son at the door: «متنزلش يا حمادة، هلمّها: الدوا والعيش والجبنة في مشوار واحد.»
- A grandmother on the phone: «يا بنتي أنا بطّلت أتصل بالبقال.. بلمّها وخلاص.»
- A student on the university steps: «هتذاكروا عندي؟ تمام، أنا هعمل لمّة: كشري وبيبسي وشيبسي.»
- The order status in the app: «اتلمّت ✓ وجاية».

**The rider: «سَبع».** «تسلم يا سبع» is what every Egyptian says to the young man who carried the bags up four floors. It is masculine, warm and aspirational, the way طيار is for Talabat. Recruiting line: «عندك موتوسيكل؟ ابقى سبع في لمّة.» (Avoid حريف, which is already the name of an Egyptian food app, Hareef.)

**Risks.** It sounds soft, not muscular, so the identity has to carry the edge (black and signal yellow, a heavy all-caps wordmark). «اتلمّ!» is also a scolding ("behave!"), and «لمّة» can mean a crowd at an accident. There are Saudi apps named Lamma, so a Gulf expansion would collide; run an Egyptian trademark search (classes 35, 39 and 43) first. In Latin it must always be written with the double M, or people read Lama or Llama.

### 2. بدري · Badri

**Why it wins.** It is Fawry's twin: فَوري/بَدري are both two syllables, both end in ي, both are words about time. It turns pillar 5, the guarantee, into a sentence nobody forgets: **«يا بدري يا ببلاش»**. It bends easily: «بدّرنالك»، «من بدري». "Order from Badri" and "order a while ago" sound the same.

**Taglines**
- «يا بدري.. يا ببلاش»
- «اطلب من بدري، يوصل بدري»
- «قبل ما تستنى»

**Habit lines**
- A wife to her husband: «لحقت تطلب العيش؟ — طلبته من بدري.»
- A mother: «الدوا قبل الأكل.. خليها على بدري، ولو اتأخر المرة الجاية ببلاش.»
- An old man at the ahwa: «زمان كنا نقول لسه بدري.. دلوقتي بنقول جه من بدري.»

**The rider: «الصاحي».** «واد صاحي» means a sharp, alert lad, and «صاحي بدري» means he got up early.

**Risks.** It is in the same family as دلوقتي, which he rejected. It sounds like a person (عم بدري, the surname, the Hindi name). Worst of all, Egyptians already say «بدري قوي!» sarcastically to someone who is late. **Every late order becomes a meme**, so only choose it if operations can keep the guarantee from day one.

### 3. بِساط · Bisat

**Why it wins.** One root, three gifts. بساط الريح is the flying carpet that brings anything (Farid al-Atrash's song for older people, Aladdin for teenagers). انبسط/مبسوط, "pleased", is said a hundred times a day. «البساط الأحمدي» means "no formalities, we're family", which is the shop-owner intimacy we have to replace. It is the only top-5 name whose **.app domain is free**.

**Taglines**
- «اطلب.. وانبسط»
- «أي حاجة.. على بساط الريح»
- «مبسوط؟ يبقى بساط»

**Habit lines**
- A grandmother after the rider leaves: «ربع ساعة؟! ده بساط ريح مش موتوسيكل!»
- A mother: «العيش والطماطم والدوا.. خليها على بساط وانبسطي.»
- A teenager: «الأكل وصل قبل الماتش ما يبدأ.. مبسوط؟ بساط.»

**The rider: «سندباد».** He has gone everywhere since the cartoon and Cairo's Sindbad funfair. Avoid «الريح»: «طلّع ريح» means passing gas.

**Risks.** «سحب البساط» means "pulling the rug" (a news cliché). It may suggest carpet shops. It sits close to Basata (بساطة), an Egyptian fintech. Fable imagery may feel "cute" to a founder who wants power.

### 4. رنّة · Ranna

**Why it wins.** It steals the competitor's own vocabulary. In Egypt the moment of delivery already *is* a رنّة: «هديك رنّة لما أوصل». And «اديني رنّة» is the cheapest, most universal phone habit there is. «ليه رنّة» also means "it has a ring to it, it has a name". It suits mixed phone literacy: one tap, like a missed call.

**Taglines**
- «رنّة.. وكله جاي»
- «من غير مكالمة.. رنّة»
- «الرنّة اللي بتستناها»

**Habit lines**
- A mother: «متتصلش بالبقال.. اعملها رنّة.»
- A son on the sofa, as the phone buzzes: «أهي الرنّة.. الأكل تحت.»
- An old woman: «أنا مبعرفش أكتب.. بس بعرف أعمل رنّة.»

**The rider: «الرنّان».** From «اسم رنّان» (a famous name), and he is the one who rings.

**Risks.** It keeps the phone call alive in people's heads, telecom brands use the word for ringtones, and in Latin it gets confused with the girl's name Rana (رنا).

### 5. مونة · Mouna

**Why it wins.** «أنا ليّا مونة عليك» means "I'm close enough to ask you anything". That is exactly why people phone the shop they know. Calling the app مونة claims that intimacy. It bends: «اتمونّ علينا». In the Levant and in older Egyptian homes, المونة also means the house's stock of provisions.

**Taglines**
- «ليك مونة علينا»
- «اتمونّ.. واطلب أي حاجة»
- «مونة البيت في مشوار واحد»

**Habit lines**
- A grandmother: «اطلبلي من مونة، أنا ليّا مونة عليهم.»
- A wife: «الحاجات خلصت؟ — خليها على مونة.»
- A sticker on a shop window: «زبوني؟ اربط محلي على مونة» (this ties in with the referral-code pillar).

**The rider: «الجار».** The shop's own delivery boy is already your neighbor.

**Risks.** مونة is also cement mortar. In Latin it reads as the girl's name Mona/Mouna, and teenagers will ask «مين منى؟». The pantry meaning is more Levantine than Egyptian. It is soft.

## 6. Recommendation and the Fayoum test

**My single recommendation is لمّة / Lamma.** It is the only name that is a verb Egyptians already use («لمّها»), names the one thing a phone call can't do (three shops, one trip), is warm for grandmothers and cool for students, and writes cleanly as LAMMA. Take Badri's best line into it as the name of the guarantee: **«لمّة — يا بدري يا ببلاش»**. Make the rider «سبع», and give the merchant app the name «رواج».

**The test: 20 people, 1 hour, one phone call (about 1,000 EGP and one afternoon).**
- **Who:** 10 women aged 45 and over (a pharmacy queue and the vegetable stalls near ميدان السواقي) and 10 people aged 17 to 24 (the Fayoum University gate).
- **What they hear:** six names read aloud once, in random order, each with the same line: «___: تطبيق بيجيبلك من 3 محلات في مشوار واحد». The six are لمّة، بدري، بساط، رنّة، مونة and عمار (the founder's best).
- **During the read:** note any smirk or laugh, and ask why.
- **60 minutes later, phone each person:**
  - Q1 (unaided recall): «فاكر اسم التطبيق؟»
  - Q2 (natural use): «لو عايز عيش ودوا، هتقول لأخوك إيه؟». Count it when the name appears inside the sentence.
  - Q3 (spelling): «ابعتهولي بالإنجليزي على واتساب».
- **A name passes with:** at least 12/20 unaided recall, at least 8/20 using it naturally in a sentence, at least 15/20 writing the same Latin spelling, and no smirk with a dirty explanation.
- **Rule:** if Lamma fails and another name passes, the street wins, not this report.
