# What the و stands for: making «واو» win

Copywriter, round 3. Starting point: the founder likes Waw and its violet + mint palette (`brand/round1/waw-board.png`).

My lens hasn't changed: **a name wins when it ends up in a sentence people already say.** Most of the risks on the table (that "wow" is kids' English, that و reads as the number ٩, sarcasm) shrink once «واو» is always *heard after an Arabic word*, never alone.

## 1. Six meanings for the و

**1. و = «وصّي» (leave word with someone you trust).**
- **Idea:** Waw is the trusted person you leave an errand with. Egyptians used this verb long before phones: «وصّيت الواد يجيب عيش وهو جاي».
- **Habit phrase:** «وصّيت واو»
- **Tagline:** «وصّي.. وصل»
- **Rider:** «سبع»
- **Risks:** the ear hears an Arabic verb first, not "wow". The ٩ problem doesn't exist in speech. Trust is the opposite of sarcasm, and the guarantee pays out when we're late.

**2. و = "and", then «وبس».**
- **Idea:** the "and" between your shops, plus the «وبس» ("that's it") that closes the list.
- **Habit phrase:** «وكمان..» … «وبس»
- **Tagline:** «عيش و دوا و خضار.. وبس»
- **Rider:** «سبع»
- **Risks:** in every ad the و sits *between words*, so the eye learns to read it as a joining word, not ٩. Grandmothers know «حرف الواو» from school.

**3. و = «وعد» (a promise).**
- **Idea:** every order is a dated promise.
- **Habit phrase:** «واو وعدت»
- **Tagline:** «واو وعد.. اتأخرنا؟ الجاية علينا»
- **Rider:** «صاحب الكلمة»
- **Risks:** it handles sarcasm best, because a late order is free. But «على الوعد يا كمّون» means "a promise never kept", and every miss becomes a joke about the brand.

**4. «واو العطف».**
- **Idea:** عطف means both "joining" and "tenderness". The letter joins your shops and carries the bags for an old woman.
- **Habit phrase:** «واو العطف»
- **Tagline:** «بتلمّ محلاتك.. وتشيل عنك»
- **Rider:** «ابن حلال»
- **Risks:** it fixes "wow" for older people (it's school grammar). But it's a grammar lesson: too clever, and students will roll their eyes.

**5. Own the "wow".**
- **Idea:** the gasp at the door when the order comes early.
- **Habit phrase:** «واو! وصل؟»
- **Tagline:** «مقولتش واو؟ الجاية علينا»
- **Rider:** «سبع»
- **Risks:** it turns sarcasm into the thing we measure. But it doubles down on English and on the grandmother problem. **Kill.**

**6. و = «وفّر» (save).**
- **Idea:** one trip instead of three.
- **Habit phrase:** «وفّرها»
- **Tagline:** «تلات محلات بتوصيلة.. وفّر»
- **Rider:** «سبع»
- **Risks:** it sits next to Wafra (وفرة), which is taken, and it has a "cheap" tone. **Kill.**

I also rejected the two words the coordinator floated. «وصل» lives on Wasla and Careem's Wasally. «ونس» is warm, but there's no errand in it.

## 2. Top 2

### #1 «وصّي واو»: the و is the verb you already use

**Why it wins.** It's the Fawry pattern exactly: verb + brand, like «ادفع فوري». And it's alliterative (و–و). «وصّى» is the pre-phone way Egyptians got errands done through a person they trust. That's the shop-owner intimacy we have to beat, now pointed at us.

**Rule:** use the verb only. The nouns «وصيّة» (a will) and «وصاية» (legal guardianship) carry death and the courts.

**Taglines**
- «وصّي واو»
- «وصّي.. وصل»
- «متنزلش.. وصّي»

**Habit lines**
- **Grandmother:** «يا بنتي متنزليش في الحر.. أنا وصّيت واو على العيش والدوا.»
- **Student:** «الكشري؟ وصّيت واو من ربع ساعة، زمانه على السلم.»
- **Shop owner, to a regular:** «المرة الجاية متتعبيش نفسك.. وصّي واو، وكود محلي SAYED.»

**In-app names**

| Element | Name |
|---|---|
| Cart | «القايمة» |
| Place-order button | **«وصّي»**, the verb on the button where the habit forms |
| Add-a-shop button | «وكمان محل» |
| Multi-shop trip | «مشوار واحد» |
| Guarantee | «وعد واو: في ميعادك.. وإلا الجاية علينا» |
| Rider | «سبع» («وصّيت السبع») |

**First billboard in Fayoum** (ميدان السواقي, white on violet, mint full stop): **«متنزلش. وصّي واو.»**

### #2 «و.. وبس»: the و is the "and" that joins your shops

**Why.** It sharpens the coordinator's idea. It turns pillar 4 into the grammar of ordering, so the order reads as a sentence: «عيش و دوا و خضار.. وبس». «وبس» also says "no hidden fees", which matters for a price-sensitive market.

**Taglines**
- «عيش و دوا و خضار.. وبس»
- «تلات محلات، مشوار واحد، ٢٠ جنيه.. وبس»
- «وكمان؟ … وبس.»

**Habit lines**
- **Grandmother:** «العيش والجبنة والدوا.. وبس، واو هتجيبهم مرة واحدة.»
- **Student:** «كشري وكمان بيبسي وكمان مناديل.. وبس. دوست.»
- **Shop owner:** «زبوني بياخد مني وكمان من الفرن اللي جنبي.. واو بتجمعهم.»

**In-app names**

| Element | Name |
|---|---|
| Cart | «القايمة» |
| Add-a-shop button | «وكمان محل» |
| Place-order button | «وبس، اطلب» |
| Multi-shop trip | «مشوار واحد» |
| Guarantee | «في ميعادك.. وإلا الجاية علينا» |
| Rider | «سبع» |

**First billboard:** **«عيش و دوا و خضار.. وبس.»** The three و's are in violet and the full stop is the mint "delivered" dot.

**#1 and #2 stack.** «وصّي واو» is how people talk about us. «وكمان.. وبس» is how the app talks back.

**Fixes for the other risks**
- **«موف»:** make the rider bag Plum Ink (`#1A0F3D`) with only the و in violet, and call the riders «سبع», never by the colour.
- **٩:** never show a lone و outside a word or a list. Keep the agency's long calligraphic tail.
- **Sarcasm:** «اتأخرنا؟ الجاية علينا» fires automatically, so the joke becomes a free delivery the customer brags about.
- **WAW in Palestine and Lebanon:** register «وصّي واو» as a slogan mark alongside the name.

## 3. Does Waw now beat Makeen?

**Yes, narrowly, and only as the pair «وصّي واو».** Through my lens, Makeen's fatal flaw was that nobody says «مكين» and it has no verb. «وصّي واو» hands people a verb they already use for exactly this errand, and it puts the brand in the object slot, the way «ادفع فوري» does. That's the strongest habit sentence any team has produced. A grandmother can say it without English, and a student can say it without irony.

Waw *alone* is still weaker than Makeen. It still has the "wow", the ٩ and the WAW apps abroad. So the win depends on discipline: the name never appears in speech or ads without «وصّي», «و» or «وبس» next to it. There's also one new risk to test. «وصّي» sounds close to «وصّلي» (Careem's Wasally), so in the Fayoum street test count how many people repeat «وصّي واو» correctly an hour later. If fewer than 8 of 20 use it unprompted in a sentence, the verb isn't sticking and Makeen is back in the race.
