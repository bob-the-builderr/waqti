# The name, from the bike seat (محمود, rider)

I looked at the Makeen, Basheer and Waw boards. There was no board for Lamma, so for it I'm judging the brief: black and yellow, with riders called «سبع».

The bag rides on my back or on the rear box for 10 hours a day. Three things matter: whether I look good, whether a car can see me at night, and whether my friends will make fun of me.

---

## Which bag would he carry with pride?

> «لو الشنطة صفرا وعليها أسود، دي أحلى حاجة وأأمن حاجة. بالليل على طريق مفيهوش نور، العربية تشوفني من بعيد. الأحمر الطوبي حلو بس بالليل بيبقى غامق. الموف؟ لأ، مش هلبسه.»

- **Lamma, a yellow bag with black lettering:** he'd be proud of it, and it's the **safest**. Yellow is the most visible color after dark on Fayoum's unlit roads. **A black bag is invisible.** Make it a yellow body with a black logo.
- **Makeen, indigo:** fine. To him it reads as "أزرق كهربا" (electric blue), and blue is a man's color.
- **Basheer, brick red:** warm and respectable, but dark at night. The small yellow dots get lost.
- **Waw, violet:** no.
- **Whichever name wins:** add **reflective strips**, and make sure the logo can be read from 10 m on a rear box, not only on a backpack.

---

## What would people call him?

| Name | What customers would say | What riders would call each other |
|---|---|---|
| مكين | «يا كابتن»، «بتاع مكين». Nobody will call a person «يا مكين». | **«المكنات»**. He likes that. |
| بشير | **«يا بشير»**: the name sticks to the rider as if it were his own. | «أنا مش بشير، أنا محمود». Some riders will hate being renamed. |
| واو | «الواو جه»، «يا واو». It sounds silly. | «يا موف». |
| لمّة | **«تسلم يا سبع»**. People already say this every day, so it costs nothing. | **«السباع»**. Every rider wants to be a lion. |

> «"يا سبع" دي إحنا بنتقالها كل يوم وبنحبها. لما الزبون يقولها وهو بياخد الأكل، ده بقشيش ببلاش.»

---

## Will friends mock any name, on the street or on TikTok?

- **واو:** yes, badly. The jokes would be «شنطة موف» (a mauve bag) and «عامل فيها واو» (showing off). And from a distance, on a moving bike, a single «و» on the bag looks like the number **٩**: «الطيار رقم ٩».
- **بشير:** a little. The bag shows only «ش», and «شين» means "ugly" in the Upper-Egypt and rural speech that Fayoum shares, so expect «شنطة الشين». And on a late order: «بشير شؤم».
- **مكين:** not because of «مكنة» but because of **«مسكين»** (poor thing). It's a one-letter rhyme kids will catch on the first day: «مكين ولا مسكين؟» and «الطيار المسكين».
- **لمّة:** mild. «بتلمّ إيه النهارده؟» (what are you collecting today?), «لمّ نفسك» (behave yourself). These are friendly jokes, not insults.

---

## Does «مكين» bring «مكنة» to mind in a bad way?

> «مكنة؟ دي مدح. "الواد ده مكنة" يعني شغّال وجامد، و"المكنة" هي الموتوسيكل. محدش هيفهمها وحش. اللي هيتريقوا بيه "مسكين".»

**Verdict:** «مكنة» is a plus. «مسكين» is the real risk.

The bigger problem is the tagline **«في ميعادك.. وإلا الجاية علينا»**. Printed on the bag, it puts the promise on **my back**. Every late order, the customer yells at the bag: «اتأخرت، الجاية عليكم!». Keep that line in the app and off the bag.

---

## Is purple or indigo manly enough for Fayoum?

> «الأزرق الغامق ده يعدّي، الناس هتقول أزرق. البنفسجي الفاتح ده لون بنات عندنا، الطيارين هيلبسوا عليه كيس أسود.»

- **Indigo (#3A2BEF): passes.** People will see it as blue.
- **Violet (#7A35FF): fails.** In Fayoum it reads as «موف», and riders will cover it. It also sits close to Breadfast's purple.

---

## His ranking

1. **«لمّة»:** yellow and black, highly visible, and «سبع» makes riders proud for free. *Condition:* the bag body must be yellow, not black.
2. **«مكين»:** strong and blue, and riders would become «المكنات». *Risks:* the «مسكين» rhyme, and the tagline must stay off the bag.
3. **«بشير»:** warm and local, but it renames the rider, it's dark at night, and the «ش» invites jokes.
4. **«واو»:** a mauve bag, «يا واو», and a «٩» on his back. He wouldn't wear it.
