# The name, as الحاجة فاطمة hears it

I looked at the three boards (Makeen, Basheer, Waw) the way she would: phone at arm's length, glasses on, and the home-screen test at 48px. Lamma has no board yet, so for Lamma she reacts to the name and the colours only.

## «مكين» Makeen: indigo + saffron

> «مَكين… زي "إنك اليوم لدينا مكين أمين"؟ كلمة محترمة. بس ودني بتسمعها "مكنة". "اطلبلي من مكين"… حاسّاها ناشفة شوية.»

- **Saying it and remembering it after an hour:** 50/50. She came back with «مكينة؟ مكنة؟».
- **«اطلبلي من مكين»:** correct, but stiff.
- **The logo:** the four-letter Kufi wordmark reads fine at 96px. At 32–48px the letters crowd together, and the saffron square over the ن looks like a stray dot.
- **The colour:** electric indigo feels like a bank or a phone company («زي شركة المحمول»). She finds it trustworthy, but cold.
- **The tagline, «في ميعادك.. وإلا الجاية علينا»:** she loves the promise. **Warning:** the round-2 plan pays a 10 EGP credit at 10 minutes late. A tagline promising "the next one is on us" will be read as a lie the first time she gets 10 EGP instead.
  > «قالوا الجاية علينا، وادوني عشرة جنيه!»

## «بشير» Basheer: brick red + sun yellow

> «بشير خير! دي بنقولها كل يوم. واللي جه بقميص سيدنا يوسف… كلمة حلوة. "اطلبلي من بشير"؟ دي سهلة، كأني بقول للواد بتاع المحل.»

- **Remembering it after an hour:** certain. She already says «بشير خير» every day.
- **It's a man's name**, and in Fayoum it's an ordinary one. For her that's a *plus*: ordering "from Basheer" feels like asking a person.
  > «"مين؟" "بشير." أفتح على طول.»
- **One caution:** she also links «البشير» with the former Sudanese president. It's minor, and only once she's thinking about it.
- **The icon:** the letter ش with three yellow dots is the most readable icon of the three at 48px.
  > «دي شين.»

  She won't know it stands for Basheer until she reads the label, but she will find it again.
- **The colour:** brick red and yellow feel «زي فخار قرية تونس، دافي، بتاع بيت». They feel trustworthy and not childish. The one risk: on the home screen, brick sits close to talabat's orange and Vodafone's red.
- **The tagline, «بشير خير.. طلبك جاي»:** she repeats it without trying.

## «واو» Waw: violet + mint

> «واو؟ دي اللي العيال بيقولوها بالإنجليزي لما يتبسطوا. "اطلبلي من واو"… من الحرف؟ مش راكبة.»

- **Remembering it after an hour:** she remembers "a letter", then has to ask which one.
- **The icon reads as the Arabic numeral ٩.** The board itself worries it might read as a "9"; for her it's worse, because ٩ is the nine she writes every day.
  > «تطبيق رقم تسعة؟»
- **The colour:** violet and mint read as children's sweets. At 48px the violet icon also sits right next to Breadfast's purple.
- **The tagline is the best of all four:** «عيش ودوا وخضار.. في مشوار واحد» says exactly what the app does, in her own words. **Keep the tagline and drop the name.**

## «لمّة» Lamma: black + yellow (no board)

> «لمّة؟ زي لمّة العيلة في رمضان. كلمة حنينة. "اطلبلي من لمّة"… ماشي.»

- **Remembering it:** yes.
- **The tagline:** «لمّها» also means "drop it, enough" in the street («لمّها بقى»), so the tagline sounds half like a scolding. «إيه اللمّة دي» also means "what's this crowd".
- **Without the shadda**, «لمة» can be misread.
- **The colour:** black is the colour of mourning («لون العزا») for her generation. A black icon also sits next to Halan's black on the home screen. Yellow on black is very readable, but feels like a warning sign, not a home.

## Her ranking

1. **بشير.** It's the easiest to say, remember and say to her son, the icon is readable, and the colours are warm and trustworthy. Its tagline is the only one she repeats unprompted.
2. **لمّة.** It's a lovely word, but it needs a non-black main colour and a different tagline.
3. **مكين.** It's respected and strong, but cold. It echoes «مكنة», and its tagline over-promises against the new credit rule.
4. **واو.** The icon reads as ٩, the name sounds like kids' English, and the colours are childish. **Its tagline should survive whichever name wins.**

**Practical note for both Quranic names:** use the word, but never print the verse on bags, stickers or receipts.
> «كلام ربنا على شنطة بتترمي في الأرض؟ لأ.»
